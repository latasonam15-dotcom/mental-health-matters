What is mental health?

Mental health is part of overall health. It affects how we think, feel, cope with stress, relate to others and function in everyday life. Everyone can experience periods of emotional difficulty, and mental health conditions can affect anyone.

Signs you may need support

  • Persistent sadness, worry, fear or irritability
  • Loss of interest or enjoyment
  • Major changes in sleep or appetite
  • Difficulty concentrating or functioning at work or school
  • Withdrawing from family, friends or usual activities
  • Feeling hopeless, worthless or overwhelmed
  • Using alcohol or other substances to cope
  • Thoughts of death, self-harm or suicide

Simple ways to protect your wellbeing

  • Talk to someone you trust.
  • Keep a regular sleep routine.
  • Stay physically active and spend time outdoors.
  • Eat regularly and stay hydrated.
  • Make time for supportive relationships and enjoyable activities.
  • Take breaks from stressful situations and screens.
  • Avoid relying on alcohol or drugs to manage distress.
  • Seek professional help early when you need it.

How to support someone

  • Listen without judging or rushing to give advice.
  • Ask how they are doing and take their concerns seriously.
  • Encourage them to speak with a doctor, counsellor or mental health professional.
  • Offer practical help and stay connected.

In an immediate crisis

If someone is in immediate danger, has seriously harmed themselves, or may act on suicidal thoughts, do not leave them alone. Remove access to immediate means where it can be done safely and seek urgent help.
